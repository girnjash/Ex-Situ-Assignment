01 — THE CHALLENGE

Pollution doesn't stop
at the water's edge.

Pollution can quietly turn a living habitat into a place where wildlife struggles to survive.

Toxic chemicals, plastic waste, polluted water and contaminated food chains can harm animals directly or weaken the ecosystems they depend on. When populations become dangerously small, conservation teams may need to protect some individuals away from the immediate threat while the wider problem is addressed.

Important: pollution is one of several pressures on wildlife. Habitat loss, hunting, invasive species and climate change can also contribute to population declines.

〰 01

Water pollution

Sewage, industrial discharge and agricultural runoff can degrade rivers, lakes and wetlands.

◌ 02

Toxic substances

Some pollutants accumulate in organisms or move through food webs, affecting health and reproduction.

⌁ 03

Plastic & waste

Wildlife may ingest plastic or become entangled in discarded materials.

☼ 04

Air & soil pollution

Contaminants can affect plants, insects, nesting areas and the wider food supply.

02 — THE IDEA

EX-SITU CONSERVATION

A safety net.
Not a substitute
for nature.

“Ex-situ” means conservation outside a species' natural habitat. It can protect individuals, maintain a carefully managed population, support research and create options for future recovery.

The long-term goal is not simply to keep animals in human care. It is to help species survive while also protecting and restoring the ecosystems they need in the wild.

04 — INDIA IN ACTION

Real work.
Real-world lessons.

India's conservation programmes show how carefully managed ex-situ work can support species protection—especially when the original threat is tackled too.

FIELD NOTE 01
Vulture conservation
Pinjore, Haryana
KEY INDIAN EXAMPLE 01 / 03

Vultures: a toxic threat in the food chain

Veterinary diclofenac, a drug used to treat livestock, was identified as a major cause of catastrophic declines in several South Asian vulture species. Vultures feeding on carcasses containing the drug could suffer fatal kidney failure.

India responded with measures including restrictions on veterinary diclofenac and conservation breeding. The Vulture Conservation and Breeding Centre at Pinjore, Haryana, is an important part of this work.

THE LESSON

Breeding birds in protected facilities can help safeguard a population—but preventing toxic exposure in the environment is essential for recovery.

A REALISTIC VIEW

Powerful tool.
Not a magic fix.

Ex-situ conservation can buy time and preserve options, but it cannot recreate a healthy ecosystem by itself.

+

What it can do

Protect vulnerable individuals, support breeding, preserve genetic resources, build scientific knowledge and sometimes enable reintroduction.

−

What it cannot do alone

Remove pollution from rivers, restore entire food webs, guarantee successful release or replace wild habitats.

◎

The best strategy

Combine ex-situ care with in-situ protection, pollution prevention, habitat restoration and community participation.
